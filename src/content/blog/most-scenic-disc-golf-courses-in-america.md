The best disc golf courses aren't just well-designed — they're set in places that make you stop walking and look up. A fairway framed by 200-foot Douglas firs. A basket perched above a mountain valley. A tee pad with the Pacific Ocean glittering through the pines.

This is the list for players who care about the view as much as the score: the **15 most visually spectacular disc golf courses in America**, organized by landscape type — mountain, coastal, woodland, and desert. Every course links to its city's FindFrolf directory so you can plan the trip.

## How We Picked These Courses

We didn't just grab the highest-rated courses (though many here are). We looked for courses where the **setting is the star** — the landscape you'd photograph even if you weren't playing. Visual impact, sense of place, player reputation for scenery, and geographic diversity across mountain, coastal, woodland, and desert.

## The Mountains

These are the courses where you're playing at altitude, where the tee pad overlooks a valley, and where the thin air lets your disc carry farther than it has any right to.

### Beaver Ranch — Conifer, Colorado

**21 holes · 8,000 feet elevation · 4.8★ (9,780 reviews)**

Beaver Ranch is the course Denver players drive 40 minutes into the foothills to play, and the view is the reason. Tee pads sit above the treeline in spots, with the Continental Divide visible to the west. Three tee sets turn the same 21 holes into a casual loop or a 7,500-foot mountain marathon. The front nine is open with mountain panoramas; the back nine tightens into pine corridors where a missed line means a 200-foot search party. $15 day pass, worth every penny.

**The shot you'll remember:** Hole 14 from the long tee, downhill 520 feet through a pine corridor to a basket framed by the mountains.

### Wondervu — Golden, Colorado

**22 holes · Foothills setting · 4.7★ (3,879 reviews)**

Wondervu is the "other" Colorado mountain course, and it shouldn't be. A 22-hole layout carved into the foothills west of Golden, with big views of the Front Range from the upper holes and tight wooded shots through the lower canyons. Less crowded than Beaver Ranch and wilder — you're sharing the hillside with mule deer. The elevation is dramatic: 300-foot climbs between some holes with front-row seats to the Rocky Mountain foothills.

**The shot you'll remember:** The tee on the upper loop, where the entire Front Range spreads out below you and you can see Golden's downtown spires in the distance.

## The Coast

Courses where the ocean is in play, where salt air mixes with pine, and where the weather changes between holes.

### Pier Park — Portland, Oregon

**18 holes · 200-foot Douglas firs · 4.7★ (9,523 reviews)**

Pier Park is Portland's backyard classic, and the scenery is the reason it's one of the most-played courses in the Pacific Northwest. Eighteen par 3s wind through a cathedral of 200-foot Douglas firs in St. Johns, with filtered light, fern-covered forest floors, and the kind of silence that makes you lower your voice.

The course is flat and walkable, which means you notice the trees more — they tower above you in every direction, and the canopy is so thick that a drizzle won't reach you for the first nine holes. It's free, it's endlessly replayable, and it's the course that makes people fall in love with Pacific Northwest disc golf.

**The shot you'll remember:** Any tee shot on the back nine, where the fairway narrows between massive firs and the light filters green.

[→ Browse Portland disc golf](/or/portland/) · [Best disc golf in Portland](/blog/best-disc-golf-courses-portland/)

### DeLaveaga — Santa Cruz, California

**27 holes · Coastal pines · 4.7★ (6,176 reviews)**

DeLaveaga sits on a bluff above the Monterey Bay, and the course has two moods: the upper holes, where you can see the Pacific through the trees, and the lower holes, where the ocean disappears and you're in tight, technical coastal forest. The signature is "Top of the World" — a 1,000-foot downhill shot from a tee pad that overlooks the entire bay.

The course is old, established, and beloved — the trees are enormous, the fairways are well-worn, and the atmosphere is pure Santa Cruz. Fog rolls in most mornings and burns off by midday, giving the course a moody, cinematic quality.

**The shot you'll remember:** Top of the World, obviously. The tee pad looks out over the Monterey Bay, and your disc disappears downhill toward a basket you can barely see.

[→ Browse Santa Cruz disc golf](/ca/santa-cruz/)

### Morley Field — San Diego, California

**27 holes · Canyon setting · 4.6★ (10,353 reviews)**

Morley Field doesn't have the ocean views, but it has something else: a canyon setting in the heart of Balboa Park that feels like a nature preserve, not a major city. Eucalyptus and palm trees frame every shot, and the fairways follow the canyon contours. It's the busiest disc golf course in the world — 10,353 reviews — and the scenery is a big part of why people keep coming back.

**The shot you'll remember:** Hole 17, a 600-foot par 4 that plays along the canyon rim with views of the city skyline to the south.

[→ Browse San Diego disc golf](/ca/san-diego/)

## The Woodland

Courses where the trees are the course — where a fairway is a corridor, where the canopy blocks the sky, and where the beauty is in the density and scale of the forest.

### Blue Ribbon Pines — East Bethel, Minnesota

**27 holes · Open-and-wooded mix · 4.8★ (8,621 reviews)**

Blue Ribbon Pines is Minnesota's crown jewel — 27 holes alternating between open meadows with ponds and tight wooded corridors where the fairway is 30 feet wide and the trees are 80 feet tall. The ponds reflect the sky on calm mornings, the wooded holes feel like playing through a cathedral, and the conditioning is immaculate. $10 day pass, on-site pro shop.

**The shot you'll remember:** The approach to the island green on the back nine, with the pond shimmering in front of you and the woods closing in behind.

[→ Browse Minneapolis–St. Paul disc golf](/mn/minneapolis-st-paul/) · [Best disc golf in Minneapolis](/blog/best-disc-golf-courses-minneapolis/)

### Idlewild — Burlington, Kentucky

**24 holes · 9,574 feet · 4.8★ (8,114 reviews)**

Idlewild is the course that makes Kentucky a disc golf destination. A 24-hole championship layout playing 9,574 feet at par 86, with fairways carved through dense Eastern forest and water hazards on a third of the holes. The hardwoods — oaks, maples, hickories — turn spectacular in fall. The signature par 5 is a 1,001-foot monster through three distinct wooded sections with a creek crossing.

**The shot you'll remember:** The drive on the signature par 5, where the fairway disappears into a tunnel of hardwoods and you have to trust your line.

[→ Browse Cincinnati disc golf](/oh/cincinnati/) · [Best disc golf in Cincinnati](/blog/best-disc-golf-courses-cincinnati/)

### Flip City — Shelby, Michigan

**18 holes · Rolling farmland · 4.7★ (4,414 reviews)**

Flip City is the legendary private course in western Michigan — rolling farmland with thick wood lines, the kind of landscape that looks like an Andrew Wyeth painting. Over hills, through tree-lined corridors, across open meadows with farm country stretching to the horizon. Private ($10 day fee, no reservations), no concessions, no pro shop — just the course and the land.

**The shot you'll remember:** The tee on the elevated back-nine holes, where the fairway drops away and the Michigan countryside rolls out below.

[→ Browse Michigan disc golf](/mi/)

### Maple Hill — Leicester, Massachusetts

**18 holes · New England terrain · 4.9★ (12,027 reviews)**

Maple Hill is the #1 course in the world by player rating, and the scenery is a big reason why. Classic New England terrain — stone walls, mature hardwoods, ponds, elevation — and in October, the foliage turns it into a postcard. Four distinct mood zones: open front with water carries, wooded middle with tight lines, dramatic back with elevation, and the finishing holes around the iconic pond.

**The shot you'll remember:** The approach to the island green on 18, with the pond, the crowd ropes, and the New England hills behind it.

## The Desert

Courses where the landscape is exposed, the views are enormous, and the beauty is in the starkness — red rock, saguaro cacti, and mountain silhouettes against a desert sky.

### Fountain Hills — Fountain Hills, Arizona

**20 holes · Lake & fountain · 4.6★**

Fountain Hills is the most photographed disc golf course in Arizona. Twenty holes around a lake, with the iconic 300-foot fountain — one of the tallest in the world — as the backdrop on nearly every fairway. Nine holes have water in play, the desert mountains frame the scene, and you're throwing over water toward baskets on peninsulas. Free, open dawn to dusk.

**The shot you'll remember:** The tee on the peninsula hole, with the fountain mid-eruption behind the basket and the mountains glowing pink at sunset.

[→ Browse Phoenix disc golf](/az/phoenix/) · [Best disc golf in Phoenix](/blog/best-disc-golf-courses-phoenix/)

### Buffalo Ridge — Phoenix, Arizona

**27 holes · Mountain desert ·**

Buffalo Ridge is the rugged counterpoint to Fountain Hills — 27 holes of elevation, blind throws, and desert terrain alongside the mountain. Saguaro-studded hillsides with panoramic views of the Valley of the Sun. Not as polished as Fountain Hills, but the scenery is wilder — the upper holes look out over the entire Phoenix metro.

**The shot you'll remember:** The tee on the high loop, where the entire Valley of the Sun spreads out below you and the sun is setting behind the White Tank Mountains.

[→ Browse Phoenix disc golf](/az/phoenix/)

### Conocido Park — Chandler, Arizona

**18 holes · Desert park ·**

Conocido is the quiet desert course that doesn't make the highlight reels but rewards the player who shows up. Flat, open, and surrounded by desert scrub with mountain views in every direction — the beauty here is in the sky. Arizona sunsets turn the mountains purple and the desert floor gold.

[→ Browse Phoenix disc golf](/az/phoenix/)

## The Ones That Don't Fit a Category

Some courses are scenic in a way that defies categorization — a mix of landscapes, or a setting that's unique to that one spot.

### Mary Moore Searight — Austin, Texas

**18 holes · Cedar corridors · 4.8★ (2,104 reviews)**

Mary Moore is Austin's crown jewel — pure Texas Hill Country scenery: dense cedar corridors, limestone outcrops, golden light. The fairways are tight, the cedar is thick, and the course feels like playing through a canyon even though you're in a city park.

**The shot you'll remember:** The downhill drive through the cedar corridor on the back nine, where the limestone bluffs frame the fairway and the Austin skyline is barely visible through the trees.

[→ Browse Austin disc golf](/tx/austin/) · [Best disc golf in Austin](/blog/best-disc-golf-courses-austin/)

### Horning's Hideout — North Plains, Oregon

**3 courses · Private property ·**

Horning's Hideout is the private three-course complex west of Portland — camp, play three entirely different courses, never see the same view twice. Canyon through a dramatic ravine, Meadow through open grassland, Lost Lake circling a private lake. The most complete disc golf experience in Oregon.

**The shot you'll remember:** The tee on the Canyon course, where the fairway drops 150 feet into the ravine and you can hear the creek at the bottom.

[→ Browse Portland disc golf](/or/portland/) · [Best disc golf in Portland](/blog/best-disc-golf-courses-portland/)

### The Canyons at Dellwood Park — Lockport, Illinois

**27 holes · Historic park · 4.8★ (9,559 reviews)**

The Canyons is the highest-rated course in the Chicago metro — a 27-hole championship layout in a historic park with hills, woods, and eight water hazards. The canyons are the star: deep, wooded ravines that the fairways cross and follow, creating a landscape that doesn't look like anything else in the Midwest.

**The shot you'll remember:** The tee on the canyon holes, where the fairway drops into a ravine and the basket is hidden behind a wall of green.

## Plan Your Scenic Trip

These 15 courses span the country, but a few clusters make for easy road trips:

- **Colorado weekend:** Beaver Ranch + Wondervu, with a warm-up at a Denver city course. Two mountain courses, 45 minutes apart, with the Rockies as your backdrop.
- **Pacific Northwest swing:** Pier Park + Horning's Hideout + Milo McIver. Forest, ravine, and river — three landscapes in three days.
- **Arizona desert double:** Fountain Hills + Buffalo Ridge. Fountain and mountain views, with a side trip to Paseo Vista for the beginner-friendly warmup.
- **New England pilgrimage:** Maple Hill + the Smugglers' Notch duo (Brewster Ridge + Fox Run). Three of the top five courses in the country, all within a four-hour drive.
- **Michigan swing:** Flip City + Kensington Toboggan. Two of the Midwest's most scenic courses, five hours apart.
