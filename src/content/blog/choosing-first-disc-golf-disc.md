Walk into any disc golf section and you'll see hundreds of discs in every color and weight. It's overwhelming. Here's the truth: you only need one disc to start, and picking it is simpler than you think. (New to the sport entirely? Start with our [how to play disc golf](/blog/how-to-play-disc-golf/) beginner's guide, then come back here.)

## The One-Disc Start

Start with a **midrange disc**. Here's why:

- **Versatile:** Handles distances from 150 to 300 feet — covers most shots you'll throw
- **Forgiving:** More stable and predictable than drivers
- **Teaches form:** Forces you to throw with technique rather than arm speed
- **Affordable:** One disc, $8–$15, done

## The Three Disc Types

| Type | Speed | Distance | Control | Beginner-friendly? |
|------|-------|----------|---------|-------------------|
| **Putter** | Slow | Short (150–250 ft) | Very high | Yes, but limited range |
| **Midrange** | Medium | Medium (150–300 ft) | High | Best first disc |
| **Driver** | Fast | Long (250–400+ ft) | Low | No — too hard to control |

Drivers look cool but they're the hardest to throw correctly. A beginner throwing a fast driver will get worse results than throwing a midrange. Save drivers for later.

## Weight and Plastic

- **Weight:** 170–175g is standard. Heavier discs handle wind better; lighter discs are easier to throw far.
- **Plastic:** Start with base plastic (cheapest, most available). It beats in faster, which actually makes it fly straighter over time.

## What You Don't Need Yet

- **A bag:** Carry your disc by hand or in a grocery bag for now
- **A full set:** One midrange covers 90% of beginner shots
- **A rangefinder:** You'll learn distances by feel
- **Specialized putters/drivers:** Add these after 10–15 rounds when you know what you like

## When to Add More Discs

After 10–15 rounds, you'll start noticing gaps in your game. That's when to add:

1. **A putter** (for close-range shots into the basket)
2. **A fairway driver** (for longer tee shots when you're ready)

Don't rush it. Master your midrange first — it'll teach you more about form than any bag full of discs.
