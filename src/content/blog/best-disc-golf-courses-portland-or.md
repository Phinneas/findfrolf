Between a championship-tested classic that hosts a PDGA event every June, a breezy hillside 18-holer with views of Mount Hood, and several state park layouts a short drive away, disc golf in Portland is as good as it gets in the Pacific Northwest. ## 1. Pier Park — The Crown Jewel of Portland Disc Golf

**Quick facts:** North Portland · 18 holes · Free · ~15 min from downtown · Intermediate–Advanced

If Portland disc golf has a home, it's Pier Park. Set in North Portland's Portsmouth neighborhood about 15 minutes from downtown, Pier Park's 18 holes wind beneath towering Douglas firs, and the course has been quietly doing it since the early 1980s. Its standing in the disc golf world gets reconfirmed every June, when the park hosts the **Rose City Open**, one of the Northwest's longest-running PDGA-sanctioned tournaments. Pros travel from across the country to test its trademark corridor shots — and spectating is free, making it the best disc golf spectator event in Portland all year.

The course itself rewards control over power. Expect mostly par 3s, tight gaps between mature trunks, and a canopy that punishes anything thrown above the ceiling. In late summer it plays fast and dry; from roughly November through May it's famously muddy, so waterproof shoes aren't optional. Amenities include restrooms, playgrounds, picnic areas, and free parking, and leashed dogs are welcome.

Beginners can absolutely play here — just bring extra discs (or a patient local). But make no mistake: no honest list of the best disc golf in Portland puts anything above Pier Park. The history, the trees, and the free access add up to the city's must-play round.

## 2. Gateway Green Disc Golf Course

**Quick facts:** East Portland · 18 holes · Free · ~12 min from downtown · Intermediate

Tucked onto a butte between I-84 and I-205, Gateway Green is the closest legitimate 18-hole course to downtown Portland — 10–15 minutes by car, or ride straight to it on the I-205 Multi-Use Path. Built out in the mid-2010s on former ODOT land, it's the stylistic opposite of Pier Park: open, breezy fairways rolling along a hillside with skyline views and, on clear days, Mount Hood looming to the east.

Expect elevation changes, some brushy rough, and wind as a genuine factor. It shares the park with a popular bike skills area, so it's common to see riders and golfers trading the same hillside. Parking is limited near the trailhead — the trade-off for being wedged between two freeways — so cycling over is a popular option.

If you've got 90 minutes and want a real round without leaving the eastside, Gateway Green is your course. And played together, Gateway and Pier make a perfect Portland day: open golf in the morning, woods in the afternoon.

## 3. Memorial Park, Wilsonville

**Quick facts:** Wilsonville · 18 holes · Free · ~25 min from downtown · Beginner-friendly

Twenty-five minutes south on I-5, Wilsonville's Memorial Park is the metro area's most forgiving 18. Fairways are open, the rough is modest, and the layout teaches without punishing — which is exactly what you want from a starter course. The park itself is spotless, with restrooms, water fountains, playgrounds, and a big free parking lot, making it the go-to for families and first-timers.

Weekend mornings can get busy with leagues and casual groups; a local's tip is weekday evenings, when you'll often have a hole or two to yourself. Bring a friend who's never thrown before — this is where Portland's disc golf classroom is in session.

## 4. Dabney State Recreation Area

**Quick facts:** Sandy River corridor · 18 holes · $5 day-use fee · ~30 min from downtown · Intermediate

Just off I-84 east along the Sandy River, Dabney packs tight, shady riverbank woods into a compact state park. The fairways are narrow, the trees are close, and the vibe is classic Pacific Northwest golf — with the bonus of a swimming beach steps from the final holes. On a hot July day, a round here followed by a river dip is about as Portland as it gets.

Dabney charges the Oregon State Parks day-use fee ($5), which also covers parking. The swim areas draw crowds in summer, but the course stays pleasantly calm. Watch your footing on the sloped ground near the water, and enjoy the shade — it's a blessing during heat waves.

## 5. L.L. "Stub" Stewart State Park

**Quick facts:** Buxton · 18 holes · $5 day-use fee · ~40 min from downtown · All levels

Forty minutes west on US-26, Stub Stewart is the rare state park designed with disc golf from day one. Its 18 holes spread across rolling meadows and regenerating forest, with more variety and breathing room than the tighter in-town layouts. It's the quietest of the courses on this list on weekdays, and the terrain is approachable enough for newer players while still interesting for experienced ones.

The real move is making it a weekend: the park has campsites and cabins, and the paved Banks–Vernonia State Trail runs nearby for hiking and biking. Play 18 in the morning, ride the trail in the afternoon, and you've got a full escape without leaving the Portland orbit.

## 6. Milo McIver State Park

**Quick facts:** Estacada · ~27 holes · $5 day-use fee · ~45 min from downtown · Intermediate–Advanced

The biggest disc golf layout in the Portland area lives at McIver, a state park above the Clackamas River canyon outside Estacada. With roughly 27 holes across varied terrain — forest, meadow, and river-edge scenery — it's the course you plan a half-day around. Ospreys and bald eagles fish the river below, and the holes along the canyon rim are among the most scenic in Oregon.

Bring water, plan for hills, and budget time: McIver isn't a quick stop, but it's the course that convinces visitors that Portland-area disc golf is legitimately elite. Pair it with lunch in tiny Estacada and you've got a perfect summer Saturday.

## Disc Golf Courses Near Downtown Portland

Short on time? Here's the metro ranked by drive time from the city center:

1. **Gateway Green — ~12 minutes.** The only full 18 you can realistically play before work.
2. **Pier Park — ~15 minutes via I-5.** The best golf inside city limits, period.
3. **Memorial Park, Wilsonville — ~25 minutes via I-5.** The easiest, most forgiving option.
4. **Dabney SRA — ~30 minutes via I-84.** Woods, shade, and a river.

No car? You're still in luck: Gateway Green sits directly on the I-205 Multi-Use Path, and Pier Park is reachable by bike via North Portland's greenway routes.

The cheat sheet: 90 free minutes → Gateway Green. A free afternoon and the best golf → Pier Park. Playing with beginners or kids → Wilsonville. A scorcher of a July day → Dabney.

## Best Free Disc Golf in Portland

Here's the good news: the three best-known in-city courses won't cost you a cent.

- **Pier Park** — free play, free parking, restrooms, and full amenities in a North Portland city park.
- **Gateway Green** — free play and free trailhead parking 12 minutes from downtown.
- **Memorial Park, Wilsonville** — free play and free parking in one of the nicest community parks in the metro.

The costs start at the state parks: Dabney, Stub Stewart, and McIver each charge a $5 day-use fee. If you'll visit three or more times a year, the Oregon State Parks annual pass (about $30) pays for itself quickly.

Also free or nearly free: spectating the Rose City Open each June at Pier Park, and most club leagues, which charge less than a fancy coffee to enter. Bottom line — you can play an entire season of quality disc golf in Portland without ever paying a green fee, and that's genuinely rare for a metro this size.

## Stumptown Disc Golf: Portland's Home Base Club

No Portland disc golf guide is complete without pointing at **Stumptown Disc Golf**, the city's homegrown club. Stumptown runs weekly leagues, bag tags, and volunteer course work days — much of what makes Pier Park special is maintained by club members — and the club helps put on the Rose City Open every June.

If you're new in town, league night is the fastest way to find card mates, learn local course etiquette, and get tips on which lines actually fly. Schedules and event calendars live on the club's website and social channels, and beginners are genuinely welcome. Joining a Portland disc golf league through Stumptown is the single best upgrade you can make to your game after buying discs.

## FAQ: Disc Golf in Portland

**What's the best disc golf course in Portland, OR?**
Pier Park. It combines history, a demanding wooded layout, tournament pedigree, and free access. If convenience matters more than challenge, Gateway Green is a close second.

**Are disc golf courses in Portland free?**
The in-city courses — Pier Park and Gateway Green — are free, as is Wilsonville Memorial Park. State park courses (Dabney, Stub Stewart, McIver) charge a $5 day-use fee.

**Is Pier Park good for beginners?**
It's playable but demanding: tight fairways and low ceilings eat plastic. First-timers should throw conservative shots, bring extra discs, or warm up at Wilsonville or Gateway Green first, then graduate to Pier.

**When is the Rose City Open?**
Every June at Pier Park. It's PDGA-sanctioned and free to spectate — check the PDGA event listing or Stumptown Disc Golf for exact dates.

**Where should a total beginner play disc golf in Portland?**
Wilsonville Memorial Park, thanks to open fairways and family-friendly amenities. Gateway Green works well too if you're staying closer to downtown.
