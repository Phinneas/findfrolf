Disc golf has no referees. There's no official walking the fairways, no replay booth, no one to appeal to — the game is self-policed, which means etiquette isn't a nice-to-have. It *is* the game's social contract. Follow the unwritten rules below and you'll be welcomed back to every course in the country; break them and you'll become the local cautionary tale.

## The golden rules of safety

**Yell "Fore!" — loudly, and immediately.** If your disc is heading anywhere near another person, yell it the second you see it. Not when it lands. Not with a polite "um, excuse me." A full-lung *FORE!* gives people time to react. If you hear it, don't look up — cover your head and duck.

**Never throw when someone is throwing or putting.** Wait for the group ahead to clear the fairway, and when you're putting, no one moves or talks until the putt is done. If someone is mid-throw on a nearby tee, you wait.

**Never walk in front of a thrower.** Even if your disc is over there. Even if they're "just practicing." Wait, or walk behind them. The polite line is: if you're downrange of someone with a disc in hand, you're in the wrong place.

## The rhythm of the round

**The furthest from the basket throws first.** It's the one rule that runs every hole: after tee shots, the player whose disc is farthest out throws next. It keeps the group moving and everyone safe.

**Let faster groups play through.** If you're a foursome behind a fast pair, wave them through at the next tee. You'll never regret it, and they'll tell their friends how great your course is.

**Don't stand on the tee while others throw.** Step off the pad, stand behind the thrower, and keep your voice down. And when it's your turn, throw when you're ready — but don't make the whole card wait while you line up a 400-foot drive like it's the final hole of the World Championship.

## Taking care of the course

**Replace your divots and don't scar the tee pads.** Spin-outs and drags chew up concrete and turf — a little care keeps the course playable for everyone.

**Don't move, break, or bend vegetation.** It's a rule violation (called a foot fault / illegal relief), and it's how you get banned from the local club's unofficial good-graces list. Play the lie as it lies, or take the penalty and move.

**Leave the park better than you found it.** Trash cans exist at most courses for a reason. Nobody loves stepping on a broken disc shard barefoot at the water hazard.

## The disc code

**Never take a disc that isn't yours.** It's not "finding" — it's theft, full stop. The local scene knows every disc and every name on them.

**If you find a disc with a number, text or call it.** The owner will be genuinely grateful. If there's no number, leave it visible at the basket or tee, or drop it in the club's lost-and-found.

**If you lose a disc in water or thick brush, you may search — but don't hold up the card.** A quick look is fine; a 20-minute wade is what flashlights and return visits are for. Mark the spot, finish the round, come back.

## Respect the course's rules

- **Check the hours.** Many park courses close at dusk or 10pm — and lit courses have their own hours. Our [course finder](/finder/) shows restrooms, hours, and lighting so you can plan ahead.
- **Leash your dog** if dogs are allowed at all — some courses (like [Blue Lake Regional Park](/or/portland/blue-lake/)) are strictly no-pets. Check before you go.
- **Pay your fees.** On pay-to-play courses like [Milo McIver](/or/portland/milo-mciver-riverbend/), the day fee is what keeps the course great. Pay it, and thank the people who run the shop.
- **Respect private property.** Church courses like [Northside Christian](/tx/houston/northside-christian/) welcome players — but not during services. Know the schedule.

## Tournament etiquette (the short version)

Tournament rounds run the same rules as casual rounds, plus: keep your card moving, don't practice putts while others are on the green, and never touch another player's disc without asking. The golden tournament rule: **be the person people hope to get paired with, not the person they dread.**

## The bottom line

Etiquette in disc golf is simple: **be safe, be patient, and treat the course and the other players the way you'd want to be treated.** The sport is one of the most welcoming on earth — because the people who play it keep it that way, one fairway at a time.
