Scoring in disc golf is straightforward once you know the terminology. Here's how it works — from casual rounds to tournament play.

## The Basics

Every throw counts as one stroke. Complete the hole, add up your strokes, and compare to par.

- **Under par:** You did better than expected (birdie, eagle)
- **At par:** You performed as expected
- **Over par:** You took more strokes than expected (bogey, double bogey)

## Scoring Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Eagle** | 2 under par (rare — usually on par 5 holes) |
| **Birdie** | 1 under par |
| **Par** | Even |
| **Bogey** | 1 over par |
| **Double Bogey** | 2 over par |
| **Triple Bogey** | 3 over par |
| **Ace** | Hole-in-one (throw-in from the tee) |

## How Par Works

Par is set by the course designer based on distance and difficulty:

- **Par 3:** Most holes (typically under 400 feet)
- **Par 4:** Longer holes (400–600 feet) or technical holes with OB
- **Par 5:** Rare — very long or multi-mando holes

A typical 18-hole course has 14–16 par 3s, 2–3 par 4s, and 0–1 par 5s, for a total par of 54–63.

## Tournament Scoring

In competitive play, scoring works the same way but across multiple rounds:

1. **Stroke play:** Total strokes across 2–3 rounds of 18 holes. Lowest cumulative score wins.
2. **Match play:** Head-to-head, hole-by-hole. Win a hole by having fewer strokes; tie = halved.
3. ** skins:** Each hole is worth a "skin" (prize). If two or more players tie a hole, the skin carries over to the next hole.

## Scorekeeping Tips

- **Keep your own score** AND a playing partner's score — compare after each hole
- **Mark your lie** before picking up your disc to avoid disputes
- **Record scores immediately** after holing out — don't wait until the next tee
- **Use a scorecard app** or paper card — don't rely on memory for 18 holes

## What's a Good Score?

- **Beginner:** +15 to +25 over par for 18 holes (totally normal!)
- **Intermediate:** Even par to +5 over
- **Advanced:** -5 to -10 under par
- **Professional (MPO):** -10 to -18 under on championship courses

Don't stress about your score early on. The goal is to have fun, get outside, and improve gradually. Track your scores over time — watching yourself improve is one of the best parts of the sport.
