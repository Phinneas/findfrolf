This is the list we built the whole directory for: the **best disc golf courses in the US** — specifically, the 20 highest-rated courses across the 15 metro areas we currently cover. Ranked by player rating from verified reviews (not one writer's opinion), and organized by region so you can plan a trip around it.

One honest note before the list: we're not claiming this is the definitive top 20 in the entire country — not yet. Courses outside our coverage, like Maple Hill in Massachusetts or DeLaveaga in California, aren't here because we haven't mapped them. What we *can* promise is that every course below is real, rated 4.6 stars or higher by thousands of players, and verified against PDGA and UDisc data.

## How We Ranked These Courses

Every course is ranked by **UDisc player rating** (the community's aggregate score), with review count as the tiebreaker. A 4.8 with 9,000 reviews outranks a 4.8 with 900 — more reviews means the score has held up over time. No pay-to-play influence, no sponsorships, just the numbers. Ratings and review counts come from UDisc's public course data, cross-checked against PDGA listings, and we refresh the list as our directory expands to new metros.

The list is organized by region — West, Midwest, South, and East — so you can find the courses closest to you or plan a road trip. Each entry lists its city and state, its verified player rating, and a one-line read on what makes it worth the drive. Use it as a checklist, a trip planner, or ammunition for the "what's actually the best course in the country" argument at your next league night.

## The West

The mountain-and-fir belt. Colorado's altitude and Oregon's old-growth timber produce two very different kinds of great golf — both on this list. Colorado's thin air adds distance to every throw; Oregon's courses reward accuracy through the trees. [Browse all Western courses](/finder) when you're ready to book a trip.

### Beaver Ranch — Conifer, Colorado

Beaver Ranch is the mountain course Denver players drive 40 minutes for: **21 holes at roughly 8,000 feet** in the Colorado Rockies, with three tee sets and tight pine lines that punish a bad hyzer. It's pay-to-play and worth a half-day. At **4.8 stars from nearly 10,000 reviews**, it's the highest-rated course in our entire directory.

Explore our [Denver disc golf directory](/co/denver), and read the full [guide to the best disc golf courses in Denver](/blog/best-disc-golf-courses-denver).

### Wondervu — Golden, Colorado

Another foothills gem outside Golden — a 22-hole mountain course with big views and bigger elevation swings. **4.7 stars from ~3,900 reviews.** Pair it with Beaver Ranch and you've got a proper Colorado disc golf weekend.

### Pier Park — Portland, Oregon

Pier Park is Portland's backyard classic: **18 par 3s** threading through 200-foot Douglas firs that come into play on almost every hole. It's free, walkable, and endlessly replayable — **4.7 stars from ~9,500 reviews**, the most-reviewed course in Oregon.

[Browse Portland disc golf](/or/portland) for the rest of the metro's best.

### Milo McIver (Riverbend East) — Estacada, Oregon

Milo McIver is Oregon disc golf's tournament cathedral — the longtime home of the Beaver State Fling. The East 18 is the marquee, playing over 8,000 feet with real elevation. **4.7 stars from ~5,600 reviews.**

Also worth exploring out West: our [Seattle directory](/wa/seattle) and [Phoenix directory](/az/phoenix), both deep with strong local favorites.

## The Midwest

The heartland holds three of the four 4.8-rated courses on this list — Minnesota and the Cincinnati area especially punch far above their population. Minnesota's parks are impeccably maintained, and the Cincinnati area hides a DGPT-level gem just across the river. [Browse all Midwest courses](/finder).

### The Canyons at Dellwood Park — Lockport, Illinois

The Canyons is the crown jewel of Chicago disc golf: a **27-hole championship layout** in a historic park with hills, woods, and eight water hazards in play. **4.8 stars from ~9,600 reviews** — the best course in Illinois, full stop.

See the rest of the [Chicago disc golf directory](/il/chicago).

### Fairfield Park — Round Lake, Illinois

A free, approachable 18 in the Chicago suburbs with a **4.6 rating from ~3,700 reviews** — proof the metro isn't just one famous course.

### Blue Ribbon Pines — East Bethel, Minnesota

Blue Ribbon Pines is Minnesota's crown jewel: **27 holes** of open-and-wooded design with ponds, doglegs, and risk-reward on almost every tee. A Timmy Gill design and a perennial top-10-in-the-country pick. **4.8 stars from ~8,600 reviews.**

[Browse Minneapolis–St. Paul disc golf](/mn/minneapolis-st-paul).

### Bryant Lake Park — Eden Prairie, Minnesota

Bryant Lake is the Twin Cities' scenic technical test — a 1999 Timmy Gill design that plays over rolling hills and woods with the lake as a backdrop. **4.8 stars from ~7,800 reviews**, with a modest $10 day-use fee at the regional park that's worth every dollar.

### Idlewild — Burlington, Kentucky

Idlewild is the Cincinnati area's professional-caliber course: a **24-hole, gold-level layout playing 9,574 feet at par 86** from the long tees. It regularly hosts the Disc Golf Pro Tour, and at **4.8 stars from ~8,100 reviews** it's one of the most respected tracks in the country.

[Browse Cincinnati disc golf](/oh/cincinnati).

### Bad Rock Creek — Liberty, Missouri

A compact technical gem in Liberty — short wooded holes mixed with open stretches, playing 3,637 feet at par 55. **4.6 stars from ~3,700 reviews.**

See the [Kansas City directory](/mo/kansas-city) for the metro's full depth.

## The South

Texas, Georgia, and the long, hot fairways where distance gets rewarded. Texas favors big arms with open fairways; Georgia mixes in the tight wooded corridors that build shot-making. [Browse all Southern courses](/finder).

### Mary Moore Searight — Austin, Texas

Mary Moore is Austin's crown jewel of wooded disc golf: **18 holes** carved through dense cedar and oak corridors where every shot demands a disciplined line. At **4.8 stars from ~2,100 reviews**, it's the highest-rated wooded course in Texas.

Read our [guide to the best disc golf courses in Austin](/blog/best-disc-golf-courses-austin), or browse the full [Austin directory](/tx/austin).

### Roy G. Guerrero — Austin, Texas

Roy G is Austin's big-arm proving ground — an open, sprawling course along the Colorado River that plays nearly **9,600 feet at par 61**. **4.7 stars from ~7,200 reviews**, and the closest thing to a tournament round inside the city limits.

### Circle C Ranch — Austin, Texas

Circle C is the free "hard" round Austin players name-drop — woodsy, rolling, with water in play at **par 64**. **4.6 stars from ~6,400 reviews.**

### Little Mulberry Park — Dacula, Georgia

Little Mulberry is the wooded, technical test Atlanta players graduate to: **18 holes of corridor golf** built for advanced and intermediate arms. **4.7 stars from ~5,100 reviews.**

[Browse Atlanta disc golf](/ga/atlanta).

### Iron Hoof — Conyers, Georgia

A free 18 outside Atlanta with a **4.7 rating from ~900 reviews** — a smaller sample size, but the score has held, and it's a locals' favorite worth the drive east.

### Harry Myers 2.0 — Rockwall, Texas

Harry Myers is the John Houck-designed gem of the Dallas–Fort Worth metro: a long, well-maintained park course with short and long holes, wooded and open stretches, and real elevation. **4.6 stars from ~5,400 reviews.**

See the [Dallas–Fort Worth directory](/tx/dallas-fort-worth). Also worth a look down South: our [Houston directory](/tx/houston) and [Nashville directory](/tn/nashville).

## The East

North Carolina owns this region on the strength of Charlotte and the Triangle — arguably the deepest disc golf state in the country. Charlotte is the sport's unofficial capital, home to the US Disc Golf Championship and more quality public courses per capita than almost anywhere. [Browse all Eastern courses](/finder).

### Diavolo at New Hope — Cary, North Carolina

Diavolo is the Triangle's championship showpiece — a long, wooded course that opened in 2020 and immediately became one of the region's most respected tracks. **4.8 stars from ~7,200 reviews**, the highest-rated course in the Carolinas.

[Browse Raleigh disc golf](/nc/raleigh).

### Hornets Nest Park — Charlotte, North Carolina

Hornets Nest is Charlotte's most famous public course: the front nine plays open parkland, the back nine dives into the wooded Piedmont. **4.6 stars from ~8,300 reviews** — and a staple of the US Disc Golf Championship's home metro.

### Bradford Park — Huntersville, North Carolina

A free 18 in Huntersville with a **4.7 rating from ~5,100 reviews** — one of the Charlotte area's most-played and best-loved courses.

### Nevin Park (Nightmare) — Charlotte, North Carolina

Nevin's Nightmare layout is exactly what the name promises: a long, almost completely wooded test that has become a proving ground for Charlotte's tournament players. **4.6 stars from ~4,800 reviews.**

See the full [Charlotte directory](/nc/charlotte) for the metro's depth.

## What Separates a 4.8 From a 4.6

Look at the top of this list and a pattern emerges. The 4.8-rated courses aren't necessarily the longest or the hardest — they're the ones that nail a complete experience:

- **Memorable design** — a signature hole, a view, or a risk-reward decision you remember after the round.
- **Conditioning** — well-maintained tees, baskets, and fairways, so the course plays as designed every time.
- **Fair challenge** — hard without being unfair, so a good shot is rewarded and a bad one is punished but never feels random.
- **A sense of place** — Beaver Ranch's mountains, Pier Park's firs, Bryant Lake's water. The setting is part of the score.

That's the honest reason a 4.8 beats a 4.6: thousands of players walked off the course thinking *"I want to play that again."*
