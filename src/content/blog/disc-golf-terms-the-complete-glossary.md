Disc golf terms are the words players use for scoring, throws, course features and gear. Most of them come straight from ball golf, and the rest come from the flight of a disc. Learn a handful and you can follow a card, a tournament broadcast or a league round without asking what anyone means.

Rule terms follow the PDGA Official Rules, and every entry has its own anchor link so you can jump straight to the word you need.

## How to Use This Glossary (A–Z Index and Jump Links)

Every term below has its own anchor link. Click a letter in the A–Z index and you land on that definition, no scrolling through sections you don't need.

### How the entries are grouped

Four groups, so you can read one and ignore the rest:

- Scoring terms
- Shot and throw terms
- Course terms
- Slang

Some terms live in two groups. "Circle" shows up under scoring and under course terms, because the 10-meter circle decides how you may putt and where the green ends. Both entries cross-reference each other.

### The entry format

One example: the 2-meter rule is "a rarely used rule stating that if the disc comes to rest more than 2 m above the ground, you must take a penalty stroke to bring it down," per Disc Golf Discovery.

### Regional variation

Wording shifts by country and club. "Frisbee golf" and "disc golf" name the same sport. And 150 class discs, which weigh less than 160g, "are the only golf discs allowed in some countries," according to El Dorado Disc Sports. If a local term is missing, treat the closest entry as your answer and check with your club.

## Scoring Terms: Par, Birdie, Eagle, Bogey, and Beyond

Par is the expected number of throws a player should need to get from the tee into the basket. Every other score name is built from that baseline.

### The Core Scores

- **Birdie**: one throw under par. What is a birdie in disc golf? It's a score of one less than par on a hole.
- **Eagle**: two throws under par. What is an eagle in disc golf? It's a score of two less than par on a hole.
- **Albatross (double eagle)**: three throws under par.
- **Bogey**: one throw over par. What is a bogey in disc golf? It's a score of one more than par on a hole.
- **Double bogey**: two throws over par.
- **Ace**: a hole in one, thrown from the tee into the basket in a single throw.

### Tournament and Round Terms

Players who don't finish a tournament get marked with "999" or "888," codes showing the score entered represents an incomplete tournament, per the El Dorado Disc Sports glossary. DFL means dead last, the player who finishes at the bottom of a tournament or division. A sandbagger, or bagger, plays in a division below their ability to win more easily. Bag tags are numbered tags club members play for, with the lowest score taking the lowest number.

### Player Ratings

The PDGA rating scale puts scratch play at the professional level at 1000, and professionals typically rate above 970.

## Shot and Throw Terms: Backhand, Anhyzer, Hyzer, Roller, and More

### Throw Types and Release Angles

**Backhand**: A grip and throw where the back of the throwing hand faces the target until the disc leaves the hand.

**Forehand (flick)**: A throw released with the palm facing forward.

**Anhyzer (anny)**: A release angle with the outer edge of the disc tilted up.

**Hyzer**: A release angle with the outer edge tilted down.

**Roller**: A throw that lands on its edge and rolls down the fairway.

**Tomahawk**: An overhead throw released with the thumb on top of the disc.

**Thumber**: An overhead throw released with the thumb under the rim.

**Overhead**: Any throw released above the shoulder toward a gap or over a tree.

### Flight Shape and Behavior

**Speed, glide, turn, fade**: The four-number system rating a disc's speed and stability.

**Overstable**: Fades hard at the end of flight.

**Understable**: Turns right on a right-hand backhand.

**Stable**: Flies straight with little turn or fade.

**Glide**: A disc's ability to hold loft.

### Putting and Approach

**Jump putt**: A putting technique for outside the 10-meter circle. It can't be used inside it (El Dorado Disc Sports).

**Falling putt**: Falling forward after a putt within 10 meters, which draws a penalty (Disc Nation).

**Parked**: Landing inside the 10-meter circle for an easy putt (Disc Golf Pro Tour).

**Approach (upshot)**: A throw meant to get close enough to putt.

**Gimme**: A putt so short it's nearly guaranteed.

### Disc Types

**Distance driver**: A disc with a speed rating of 9 to 14 that needs real arm speed (BWBDA).

**Midrange, approach disc, putter**: Slower discs for shorter shots and greens.

**Brick**: A disc that doesn't glide well and drops fast.

**Bead**: A ridge on the bottom rim of a disc.

**150 class**: Discs under 160g, the only legal golf discs in some countries (El Dorado Disc Sports).

If this were my call, I would learn a hyzer and an anhyzer before chasing a roller. Everyone online tells new players to buy a distance driver first. Here's the problem: a speed 9 to 14 disc won't fly for most beginners. I would start with a midrange.

## Course Terms: Tee, Fairway, Circle, OB, and the Green

### Layout

The **tee box** is where each hole starts. Between the tee and the basket runs the **fairway**, the mown route. The **green** is the area around the basket, typically within 10 meters (F109). Everything off the fairway is the **rough**.

The **10-meter circle** is the line that changes how you putt. It sits 10 meters, or 32 feet and about 9 ¾ inches, from the basket (F2). No circle painted? Group consensus decides whether your disc is inside it (F3).

### Rules and Penalties

**OB (out of bounds)** means your disc came to rest outside the playing area, and you add a penalty stroke. The PDGA Official Rules glossary covers OB, stance, and the rest of these.

The **2-meter rule** is rarely used. If your disc rests more than 2 m above the ground, you take a penalty stroke to bring it down (F111).

An **approximate lie** is a lie the group establishes to resume play after a delay, a lost disc, or a misplay.

On your next throw, your foot must be within 30cm of the mini (F13). I would mark every lie, even casual ones. The common advice is to skip the mini in casual rounds, but that habit costs you in league play.

### Basket and Distance Zones

The **basket** catches discs with **chains** around a central **pole**. A **bounce-out** is when a disc hits the pole hard enough to bounce back out (F1 source).

Inside the circle, you must show balance after releasing the putt and can't fall forward. The zone from 10 to 20 meters (33 to 66 feet) is the longer approach range (F85). The **away** player, farthest from the basket, throws next.

## Disc Golf Slang and Nicknames: What Golfers Really Say

Disc golf slang is the shorthand regulars use on the course, and the biggest one is what people call the sport itself. Among friends you'll hear "frisbee golf," "frolf," or just "golf." Most clubs have their own private lingo on top of that.

![Diagram: Disc Golf Slang and Nicknames: What Golfers Really Say](/diagrams/napkin-51b6b7cb57.png)

### Common Slang Terms

- **Park job:** A throw that lands so close to the basket it's essentially a tap-in. The Disc Golf Pro Tour glossary calls this "parked," meaning a disc that lands "within the 10-meter circle, making for an easy putt."
- **Spit out:** A putt that hits the chains and pops back out instead of sticking.
- **Chain music:** The sound of a disc crashing into the chains, usually followed by cheering.
- **Tree magnet:** A disc that seems drawn to every trunk on the fairway.
- **Shank:** A badly missed throw that goes nowhere near the intended line.
- **Griplock:** When your grip holds the disc too long and the shot sails wide right or left.
- **"Nice":** The one word every disc golfer wants to hear after a good throw. It's short and flat, and it means the shot was genuinely good.

### Etiquette Slang: Found a Disc

If you find a disc with no name or number, drop it in the course lost-and-found or ask around the group ahead. I would never pocket it silently, even on a busy Saturday. That's the one bit of slang etiquette worth taking literally.

### When the Usual Answer Doesn't Apply

Nicknames vary by region and club. "Frisbee golf" is common among casual players, though some clubs treat it as a gentle ribbing. If a term confuses you, ask. Nobody minds.

## FAQ

### What is a disc golf goal called?

The goal in disc golf is the basket, a device designed to catch a disc by using a number of chains to stop its flight. You'll also hear it called the pin or the target. On the course, most players just say basket.

### What are the three different types of throws in disc golf?

You'll hear three throw names most often: backhand, forehand, and overhead. A backhand is a grip and throw where the back of your grip hand generally faces the basket until the disc leaves your hand. The forehand, also called a flick, releases with the palm facing the target. Overhead throws send the disc above your head. The roller is a separate ground shot that lands on its edge and rolls. I would learn the backhand first, then add a forehand for holes that curve the other way.

### What are some common nicknames for disc golf?

Frisbee golf is the most common nickname, typically used by recreational players. You'll also hear frolf, disc, and ball golf's cousin jokes. The PDGA is the Professional Disc Golf Association, the governing body for disc golf, and that acronym shows up constantly in conversation. Among friends, plenty of players just call it golf and let context do the work. None of these nicknames change the rules. If you're new, say disc golf and you'll be understood everywhere.

### Hypothetical: You're playing a course you've never played before. You can only bring 5 discs. What are you bringing?

I would carry a putting putter, a throwing putter, a mid-range, a stable fairway driver, and one distance driver. That covers short putts, approach shots, and tee shots without forcing a shot I can't shape. The 10-meter circle is the circle around a basket that indicates how a player may putt, so a putter you trust inside that circle matters more than a fourth driver. If this were my call, I would leave the extra distance driver at home and take a second mid-range instead.

### Disc Golf sayings?

Most sayings you hear are short and tied to scoring. Nice park job follows a disc that lands close to the basket, typically within the 10-meter circle, making for an easy putt. Players shout ace when a disc goes in from the tee box in one throw. You'll also hear get in the hole, chains, and that is a gimme. My favorite is a plain "good comebacker" after a missed putt, because it says the next one is makeable. Sayings are slang, not rules, so use whichever fits your group.
